Design system · illustration and voice

One pencil, one yellow

Every drawing is graphite line plus one flat yellow fill, printed slightly off-register, like a colouring book that slipped. Nothing is shaded, nothing has a face, and every object stands on the same floor line.

The drawing rules

Canvas 160 × 120 (the done state is 160 × 150). Floor line at y = 104, always drawn, always graphite. Line weight 2.2 with round caps and joins; the detail weight is 1.6. Three to six closed shapes per scene.

The fill is the same shapes, shifted 5 right and 4 down, no stroke, in --fill. A hand wobble comes from one SVG filter (turbulence 0.03, displacement 2.6) applied to the line group only, so the paper stays flat and the pencil looks held.

Hazards are shown by circling them with a graphite loop over a thick yellow marker stroke, never by tinting them red. Objects are drawn from the side, at a child’s height. No people, no faces, no babies.

The six rooms

Living room

A TV on its console held by a strap to the wall, an armchair. The first fix most homes need.

Bedroom

Bed, pillow, bedside table and lamp. Window stops and cord fixes live here.

Nursery

Crib with slats and a mobile, plus the dresser that needs anchoring.

Kitchen

Upper and lower cabinets, a pot with its handle turned in, a fridge.

Bathroom

Tub with a ripple of water line, faucet, a stool and towel.

Stairs

Five steps up to a gate at the top. The gate gets the fill, so the fix is the picture.

The three states

Empty

An empty frame, a pencil on the floor, a dotted tick where the first mark will go. Used on Welcome and any list with nothing in it. “Nothing marked yet. The first tick is yours.”

All done

The frame fills when a room completes: one tick per task, the top one marked in yellow. Name and date go underneath in type, like writing beside the mark. Played once, never looped.

Photo scan

Your photo is shown as a pencil trace with camera corners, and each thing we spotted is circled in marker. Real photos of a home never appear in the product’s own artwork.

In dark mode

Voice

Calm, competent, warm: the neighbour who has done this before and tells you plainly. Specific nouns, honest times, no drama. We describe the fix before the worry, and we never name an injury.

Rules

Name the thing and the time. “Anchor the TV, about 10 minutes.”

Say what to do. Start every task with a verb. Explain why in one plain sentence.

No exclamation marks, no “Oops”, no “Keep going”. Progress is a fact: “9 of 24 fixes done.”

Never scare, never lecture, never diagnose. No statistics, no medical claims. Medical questions go to Poison Help, 911 or the pediatrician, and the app says so.

Errors say what to do next and whose fault it isn’t. Currency and times are ranges, labelled “usually”.
